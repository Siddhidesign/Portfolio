Research Lens

Self-initiated · Nov 2026 · MCP server
input · the problem

AI synthesis that cannot tell you who said it, or how many people said it

→
output · the result

An MCP server that answers research questions with participant-level citations attached

The usual AI research workflow is copy and paste, so the model only sees what you happened to paste and the output carries no citations. I built an MCP server in TypeScript that lets an assistant query a research repository directly. Five tools, and one deliberate constraint: the server retrieves and counts, it does not interpret. Every excerpt returns with a participant ID, round and timestamp. pattern_frequency counts distinct participants rather than quotes, because three quotes from one person is not a pattern. draft_findings ends with a NOT SUPPORTED section that refuses causal claims on a small sample. The researcher still writes the finding.

Money Anchor

Master’s capstone · UX researcher and designer · 2025 to 2026
input · the problem

People who overspend first and regret later

→
output · the result

An AI budgeting coach that speaks up before you spend

An IRB-approved mixed-methods study (protocol HUM00280873). 40+ research papers summarized and analyzed with AI, then 24 participants in interviews and focus groups shaped the coach. 5 sections sit in a bottom bar, like a bank app, so there was less to learn. rules engine · UX laws

LAW 01 OF 06

Jakob’s law

People expect your product to work like the ones they already use.

FAMILIAR ORDER FLOW

  1. 1. Find customer
  2. 2. Add items
  3. 3. Check stock
  4. 4. Confirm
  5. 5. Invoice

NOVEL ORDER FLOW

  1. 1. Invoice
  2. 2. Confirm
  3. 3. Find customer
  4. 4. Check stock
  5. 5. Add items
Same five steps. Only one order matches how staff already work.

TRY ITSame five steps, two orders. Which one could you do without thinking?

TRIED AND TESTEDEV 2024EV charging appFound in testing · 10 drivers
109.66 sfor experienced drivers to charge and pay, the slowest task in testing109.66 s for experienced drivers to charge and pay, the slowest task in testing

Experienced drivers expected payment to work like Tesla’s: set up once, then automatic. Ours didn’t, and it showed. After testing, payment moved to a default method, with the pay button always on screen.

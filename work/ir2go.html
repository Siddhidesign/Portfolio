Making the IRS app easy to use

Role
UX/UI Designer
Timeline
Winter 2025, 4 months
Team
5 students
I owned
Research, refund and payment flows, accessibility
Problem
IRS2Go was rated 2.8 stars, 45% of people quit before finishing a task, and no screen met WCAG 2.1 AA.
What I did
Mined 350+ reviews and posts, interviewed about 10 taxpayers, and redesigned the 3 tasks people open the app for.
Result
Usability score went from 31.3 to 78.25, and every redesigned screen passed WCAG 2.1 AA.
  • 31.3 → 78.25usability score (SUS)
  • 350+reviews and posts analyzed
  • 100%screens pass WCAG 2.1 AA
  • In-apppayment, no trips to the website

01The problem

People open IRS2Go to check a refund, pay, or find free help. Forms rejected correct details, payments bounced out to the website, and help didn't cover refunds or payments.

02Research

I analyzed 150+ App Store reviews and 200+ Quora posts, interviewed about 10 taxpayers, ran a survey, and summarized related papers with AI. Then we wrote out every step of each task to see where it broke.

What we found

  • Tax Year looked like a text box but was a list.
  • The refund amount had to match to the cent, with no example.
  • Payments sent people out to the website, and some still couldn't pay.

03Key decisions

1Help before the error

WhyThe old form explained its rules only after people got them wrong. Now every field has a label and an example.

✓ Part of the 78.25 redesign

2The answer as a timeline

Why"Return Received" gave no date and no next step. Received, Approved, Sent shows where you are and what's next.

✓ Part of the 78.25 redesign

3Pay without leaving the app

WhyBeing sent to the website mid-payment broke trust, so every option and its fee sits in the app.

✓ No trips out to the website

04What I rejected, and why

  • Rejected

    Log in first, like the old app

    Many people quit at the login screen just to check a refund. "Explore without signing in" became the main button, and nobody dropped off there.

  • Rejected

    Pay straight from the form

    3 of 6 testers hesitated without a summary. A confirmation screen with the total before paying took that to 0 of 6.

Problem Statement

An official app people gave up on

People open IRS2Go to check a refund, pay, or find free help. Forms rejected correct details, payments bounced out to the website, and help didn't cover refunds or payments.

Possible Solution

Mined 350+ reviews and posts, interviewed about 10 taxpayers, and redesigned the 3 tasks people open the app for.

Objectives & Goals

01

No dead ends

Every task finishes inside the app.

02

Right the first time

Explain each field before people get it wrong.

03

Open to everyone

No account to check a refund, and accessible to all.

Target Audience

  • Taxpayers checking a refund
  • Taxpayers making a payment
  • People looking for free tax help

Key insights derived

  1. Tax Year looked like a text box but was a list.
  2. The refund amount had to match to the cent, with no example.
  3. Payments sent people out to the website, and some still couldn't pay.

The insight we designed around

Rules you learn by failing

The old form explained its rules only after people got them wrong: Tax Year looked like a text box, and the refund amount had to match to the cent.

User scenarios

Refund status

A young adult filing taxes for the first time

He files on the IRS website, then installs IRS2Go to keep checking his refund. He enters his details from the dropdowns and submits to see where his refund stands.

Payment

The same taxpayer, now owing money

After checking his refund he realizes he owes the IRS. He opens Payment, picks a method, enters his details and confirms the transaction.

Tax help

A senior citizen with a pile of tax documents

Confused by the paperwork, he opens Tax Help, finds the Free Tax Prep Site Locator, enters his ZIP code and sees nearby locations on a map.

Ideate Phase

Low-fidelity sketches

Plot twist

Many people quit at the login screen.

They wouldn't log in just to check a refund. "Explore without signing in" became the main button, and nobody dropped off there after the change.

Ideas I tested and dropped

  • Dropped

    Pay straight from the form

    3 of 6 testers hesitated without a summary. A confirmation screen with the total before paying took that to 0 of 6.

High-Fidelity Design

Three tasks that now finish inside the app

01

Every field explains itself before you type

Each field has a label and an example, and a sample form shows where each answer is on your return, so people don't learn the rules by failing.

✓ Part of the 78.25 redesign

Redesigned refund form with labels, examples and a sample form.

02

Your refund reads like a timeline

Received, Approved, Sent replaces a bare "Return Received", so people see where they are and what comes next.

✓ Part of the 78.25 redesign

Refund status: Received, Approved, Sent.

03

Pay without leaving the app

Every payment option and its fee sits side by side in the app, instead of a jump to the website mid-payment.

✓ No trips out to the website

Payment screen comparing fees by card type.

Design Quality

The reasoning behind the screen: visual design, accessibility and the design system it was built on. Contrast figures are measured from the final screens; any gap is fixed in the Figma file and shown before and after.

  • Visual design
  • Accessibility
  • Design system
01

Visual design

  • IRS navy is the only accent; everything else stays neutral.
  • One filled button per screen, with help placed next to the question it explains.
02

Accessibility, measured

  • Body text 6.7:1 and links 12.8:1 on white.
  • A visible label above every field; every tab pairs an icon with a word.
  • Fixed in Figma: inactive tab labels 2.2:1 → 5.6:1 (#5E6873) and placeholders 2.3:1 → 4.8:1 (#6E7072). The gray Get Status is the disabled state, which WCAG exempts; filled in, it turns IRS navy at 12.8:1.
03

Design system

  • Apple's tab bar, navigation bar, form fields and pickers, so it behaves like any iPhone app.
  • IRS brand colors on top keep it feeling official.
Design system usedApple Human Interface Guidelines, in IRS colours

It's an iPhone app, so the tab bar, navigation, form fields and pickers follow Apple's guidelines. The IRS brand colours keep it feeling official. Usability score (SUS) went from 31.3 to 78.25.

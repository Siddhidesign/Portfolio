In 30 seconds

The project, the problem and the fix

01

Project

Fit Check, a concept redesign of the AI shopping assistants on Best Buy, Walmart, Home Depot and Target.

02

Problem

They chat instead of helping: deals before questions, needs retyped, ads mixed into the picks, and the shopper still does the checking.

03

How I solved it

I turned the chat into a task. The assistant asks only what changes the answer, checks each pick in view, compares side by side, and ends in a checked cart the shopper commits to.

04

Design system

The retailers' internal systems aren't public, so I built one component library on Material Design 3 and other public guidance, then skinned it with each store's public brand colours.

05

Result

12 steps and 5 edge cases per retailer in a clickable prototype. Concept, not tested with shoppers.

Design system

Built on public design systems

I couldn't use Best Buy's, Walmart's, Home Depot's or Target's internal design systems: none of them is public. So I built on widely used public ones, and let each one decide one thing.

One set of Figma variables carries a mode per retailer that swaps only brand colour, action colour and corner radius. Layouts and components never change, so a team could ship one system across four brands.

Foundation

Material Design 3

Components, states and tokens: chips, buttons, slider, switch, cards, progress.

AI behaviour

Microsoft HAX Toolkit

Say what it can do, clear up intent before acting, explain why, make correction easy.

Trust and failure

Google PAIR

Show uncertainty and plan graceful failure: Unknown and Check required are real states.

AI labelling

IBM Carbon for AI

The AI badge opens what it used and how it picks.

Shopping and usability

Baymard and NN/g

Compare tables, add-ons, totals with tax, response times and loading.

Accessibility

WCAG 2.2 AA

Contrast, 44 px targets, visible focus, a tap for every drag, help in one place.

Design

Twelve steps per retailer, six shown here

01

Ask only what changes the answer

The product need, the budget and how to get it, on one screen. Known answers arrive filled in with their source; anything skipped uses a stated default.

✓ Built on all 4 retailers

Ask Blue's quick check on one screen: how you want to listen, the budget filled in from the goal, and pickup today filled in from your store, each with its source.

02

Checked picks, ranked by fit, no ads

Each card lists the checks it passed against the shopper's own answers. A pick with only 2 ratings says so.

✓ Built, not tested

Two laptops that match, each with the checks it passed, a ratings count flagged as too few, and pickup stock with the time it was checked.

03

Compare side by side, and say Not stated

The panel widens into a table. Differences are tinted and labeled, Only show differences hides the rest, missing facts read Not stated, and the total includes tax.

✓ Built on desktop, tablet and mobile

Mobile compare table: rows that differ are highlighted, Graphics reads Not stated in listing, and the total includes estimated tax.

04

Check required, instead of a confident guess

When the listing doesn't state the warranty, an amber card says so and offers an associate, with the source and the date checked.

✓ Edge case E1, built

On the product page an amber Check required card says the listing doesn't state the warranty, with Chat with an associate and Why we say this.

05

Ask about the add-on; the button says what goes in

Protection, cables and mounts are a question, not a default. The button reads Pick one to continue until the shopper chooses, then Add 2 items.

✓ Built on all 4 retailers

Before adding to cart: the optical cable, I already have one and Not now, nothing pre-selected, and the button reading Pick one to continue.

06

A person one tap away, with the context passed

The associate gets the goal, the item and the open question, so the shopper never repeats themselves.

✓ Edge case E3, built

Chat with an associate: the goal, the item and the open question are listed, then Chat now, Call me or Book in store.

Iterations

How it got here, and what I dropped

Plot twist

My first version looked finished. It was wrong twice.

First, the NN/g and Baymard usability studies and the 2026 shopper surveys showed what my own review had missed: an unlabeled launcher, no sign of what the assistant could see, nothing marked as unverified, no way to reach a person. Version 4 fixed each one. Then I audited version 5 against Microsoft's HAX guidelines, Google PAIR and WCAG 2.2 and found a form dressed as a chat: four questions, one per screen, in boxy tiles. Version 6 asks together, fills in what it knows, and puts every choice in the chat style shoppers already use.

Ideas I tested and dropped

  • Dropped

    Adding to the cart from inside the chat

    It hid the price breakdown and the add-ons. The assistant now guides to the product page and stages the add, so the shopper sees the total before anything moves.

  • Dropped

    One question per screen

    It felt careful but read as a form, and the plan stayed locked until every answer was in. Asking together, with known answers filled in, still clears up what the shopper means before acting (HAX guideline 10) in one screen.

  • Dropped

    Questions the assistant could answer itself

    Room size, must-have specs and wall or stand either follow from what it knows (a bedroom TV means a small room) or don't change which product fits. Mounting moved to the add-on step.

  • Dropped

    Square tiles for goals and checks

    They read like a settings page. Goals and Check before you decide now use the chat bubble and rounded suggestion pills each retailer's assistant already uses, so every screen feels like one conversation.

Results

Reviewed through a developer, business and UX-writing lens, revised against published research, then audited against published design principles. Nothing here has been tested with shoppers yet.

Did it meet the goals?

Finish the task

Built: every flow ends in a checked cart, with the order placed by the shopper

Ask only what matters

Built: 3 questions on Best Buy, Walmart and Home Depot, 4 on Target, known answers filled in

Check, don't guess

Built: Check required, Not stated and Taking longer states, a source on every claim

Stay in control

Built: nothing pre-selected, Stop on every search, an associate one tap away

What I learned

Polish hides gaps, and so does care. The research found problems my review missed, and the principles audit showed that asking one question at a time, which felt careful, was really a form. Most fixes were labels and defaults, not features.

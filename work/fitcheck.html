Problem Statement

Four assistants, the same task, the same four problems

In 2026 Best Buy, Walmart, Home Depot and Target all shipped AI shopping assistants: Ask Blue, Sparky, Magic Apron and Ask Target. I gave each one the same task, a gaming laptop under $2,000, and recorded every step. All four showed deals before asking a question, made me retype my specs, mixed ads into the picks, and dropped the conversation the moment I opened a product page.

Published research says shoppers feel this too. In 2026 surveys, 54% of people who shopped with AI said they double-checked everything it told them, and 78% assumed brands pay their way into the picks. Nearly half of US shoppers who bought on an AI recommendation have returned the item (Blue Yonder, 2026). The retailer, not the model, takes the blame.

Possible Solution

Recorded all four assistants on the same task, redesigned the 12 steps inside each one on its real pages, then revised every step against 2025 to 2026 usability research on retail assistants.

Objectives & Goals

01

Three taps before any pick, so the assistant ranks by what the shopper needs.

02

Stay in control

Nothing enters the cart, and nobody loses the chat, without the shopper's say.

Empathize Phase

Qualitative research

Desk research first: the 2025 to 2026 usability studies of live retail assistants (Nielsen Norman Group, Baymard) and the shopper surveys on AI trust (Gartner, Klaviyo, Blue Yonder, Synchrony). Then my own recordings of the four assistants on one task, scored against NN/g's chatbot guidelines.

Observations

54%of AI shoppers double-check everything it says (2026 survey)
78%assume brands pay their way into AI picks (2026 survey)
11%at most would let AI make the purchase (Gartner, Jan 2026)

Key insights derived

  1. Every assistant answered before it asked. Deals and prompts came first; my budget and must-haves came only after I typed them.
  2. The shopper did the checking. Fit, stock, warranty and price were left for me to verify on the product page.
  3. The chat got lost. Opening a product page closed or reset the conversation on all four.
  4. No reason to trust. Picks rarely said where a fact came from, and sponsored items looked like recommendations.

Ideate Phase

Plot twist

My first version looked finished. It was wrong in six places.

Reading the NN/g and Baymard usability studies and the 2026 shopper surveys showed what my own review had missed: an unlabeled launcher, no sign of what the assistant could see, nothing marked as unverified, no way to reach a person. Version 4 fixed each one, on desktop, tablet and mobile.

Ideas I tested and dropped

  • Dropped

    Adding to the cart from inside the chat

    It hid the price breakdown and the add-ons. The assistant now guides to the product page and stages the add, so the shopper sees the total before anything moves.

High-Fidelity Design

Twelve steps per retailer, six shown here

01

Three taps before any pick

Use, must-haves and budget as chips. No typing, no guessing what the assistant needs to know.

✓ Built on all 4 retailers

Ask Sparky asks what the laptop is for, the must-haves and the budget as tap chips, then Show matches.

02

Checked picks, ranked by fit, no ads

Each card lists the checks it passed against the shopper's own answers. A pick with only 2 ratings says so.

✓ Built, not tested

Two laptops that match, each with the checks it passed, a ratings count flagged as too few, and pickup stock with the time it was checked.

03

Compare side by side, and say Not stated

The panel widens into a table. Rows that differ are highlighted, missing facts read Not stated, and the total includes tax.

✓ Built on desktop, tablet and mobile

Mobile compare table: rows that differ are highlighted, Graphics reads Not stated in listing, and the total includes estimated tax.

04

Check required, instead of a confident guess

When the listing doesn't state the warranty, an amber card says so and offers an associate, with the source and the date checked.

✓ Edge case E1, built

On the product page an amber Check required card says the listing doesn't state the warranty, with Chat with an associate and Why we say this.

05

Ask about the add-on, nothing pre-selected

Accident protection is a question, not a default. The all-in total sits next to the budget before anything is added.

✓ Built, not tested

Before adding to cart: the accident plan options, No plan included, nothing pre-selected, the all-in total with tax against the $2,000 budget.

06

A person one tap away, with the context passed

The associate gets the goal, the item and the open question, so the shopper never repeats themselves.

✓ Edge case E3, built

Chat with an associate: the goal, the item and the open question are listed, then Chat now, Call me or Book in store.

What I learned

Polish hides gaps. The published research found problems my own review did not, and most of the fixes were labels, not features.

One app for every charger

Electric cars have the range. What fails drivers is the software: too many apps, RFID cards, and chargers that turn out to be broken. I designed one app that finds a working charger and pays for it.

The short version

The problem

It's really a trust problem

Drivers can't tell if a charger works until they get there, and every network wants its own app or card.

What I did

Research, audit, design

Compared research papers, industry reports and owner forums, audited 4 apps task by task, then designed 4 core flows.

The result

One flow, start to finish

Find a working charger, drive there, charge and pay in one app, with no RFID card. It's a concept, so there are no launch numbers.

How I did it

Five steps, from forums to final screens

01

Research: three sources, one pattern

Research papers, industry reports and EV owner forums all pointed to the same top problems: broken chargers with no warning, confusing payment across networks, and no safety or amenity info at stations.

S

Meet Sam, 31

She commutes 18 miles a day in her electric car with no trouble. Now she's planning her first 80-mile road trip, and she has only ever charged at home.

  1. No way to tellThree stations on the map, no ratings, no sign of which ones work.
  2. Broken chargerShe drives 38 miles to an "Out of Service" sticker.
  3. Another appThe next charger needs a different app and a membership: 12 minutes in a parking lot.
  4. Just makes itShe arrives with 11% battery and takes the same road home out of caution.
02

Audit: where today's apps fall short

  • EVgo: some stations still need an RFID card, plus a subscription.
  • ChargePoint: big network, confusing pricing, no cross-network payment.
  • Tesla: works beautifully, but only for Teslas.
  • Every network: no reviews, safety or amenity info at the station.

No app closes the loop from finding a charger to paying for it. That gap became the product.

03

Ideas: sketch first, then pick

I sketched by hand in four groups: live data, one payment, personal suggestions, and amenities and safety. Then I kept four features:

  • Battery health over time, so charging habits are visible.
  • Low-battery alert that points to the nearest working charger.
  • One payment that works on every network.
  • Live reviews on reliability, safety and amenities.
04

Flows: the four core tasks

Each feature maps to one task, mapped step by step before any screen was drawn.

05

Final design: readable in two seconds

People use this standing at a charger, one-handed, often at night. So color carries the meaning first: green works, red doesn't, amber is unknown.

Learnings

What this taught me

Learning 1

Range anxiety is about trust

Reliable information fixes it, not bigger batteries.

Learning 2

Glanceable beats dense

At a charger, people need the answer in one look, not more data.

Learning 3

Drivers trust drivers

Reviews from other owners feel more reliable than what the network says.

"If I knew the charger was going to work before I drove there, I wouldn't need 80% of the anxiety-management features I've invented for myself."

EV owner, interview participant

01Input

The problem

Too many apps, and no way to know

Each charging network wants its own app, card or membership. Drivers can't tell if a charger works until they arrive, and no app says whether a station feels safe at night.

Halfof 3,300 drivers worried about range
25%hit unavailable or incompatible chargers
15%+felt unsafe at a station

From Plug In America's 2023 driver survey and J.D. Power's 2023 public charging study, which we reviewed.

WhyRange anxiety isn't only about the battery. It's about not trusting the information.
02Data in

Research at scale

16,767 reviews, studies and apps

We collected 16,767 Google Maps reviews of US charging stations. Alongside them, we reviewed peer-reviewed studies and industry reports since 2012, summarized with AI, plus EV owner forums, and compared Tesla, ChargePoint, Electrify America, PlugShare and EVgo.

$ load Google_Map_EVCS_review_data # 16,767 reviews
› drop empty, duplicate, closed-station and under-20-character reviews
› keep reviews from the last 12 months, US only
› tag each review by network with AI-assisted Python in Google Colab
› sample 80 reviews per network: 400 to compare fairly
› average rating: Tesla 4.26, ChargePoint 3.76, others 3.25 to 3.34
  • No app put safety and amenities first.
  • Apps that cover every network, like PlugShare, had information but no way to pay.
  • Network apps took payment, but only on their own network.
03Analyze · AI

Synthesis

Four themes from the data and the papers

Range anxiety

The fear of running out of charge, worst on long trips.

Reliability

Charging speed, compatibility, and whether a charger works.

Payment

A different app or card for each network.

And a fourth: safety and amenities, lighting, restrooms and food nearby. Tesla's higher ratings pointed at what drivers expected everywhere: chargers that work and payment that just happens.

04Design

Decisions log

Familiar maps, clear status, one wallet

Each of us sketched 4 ideas in 4 minutes, and we voted on the features to keep. We built the screens in Figma, guided by 4 usability rules: familiar patterns like Google Maps, clear system status, user control, and preventing errors, like checking that a charger fits your car.

decision 1tested

One payment for every network

Each network wanted its own app, card or membership. Here you check in and pay in one place, with no RFID card.

The hardest task in testing, so it changed afterwards.

decision 2tested

Show what's around the charger

Many drivers, especially women, worry about safety at quiet stations, and families need places to stop. So every station shows what is around it before you drive there.

Testers praised it across the board.

decision 3tested

Plan the trip, chargers included

Range anxiety peaks on long trips in unfamiliar places. So you enter where you're going and see chargers along the route, with live traffic.

New EV drivers praised how simply it previews stops.

05Verify · me

Usability testing

10 drivers, 3 tasks

We tested with 10 people: 5 new to EVs and 5 experienced drivers. Each found a nearby charger, found the battery health page, and charged and paid, thinking out loud while we timed them. Then everyone filled in the SUS survey.

new to EVs89.5
experienced68.0
everyone78.75
Jakob's law · changed after testingnot tested again

Pay like the apps drivers already trust

Experienced drivers took 109.66 seconds to charge and pay, and rated it 3.66 out of 5. They expected payment to work like Tesla's: set up once, then automatic. So payment now uses your default method, and a pay button stays on screen.

changed after testingnot tested again

Words people actually use

"Phantom battery" confused people, so it became "stand-by", and the battery page got a simpler layout.

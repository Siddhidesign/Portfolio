01Input

Business and user goals

The website is the manufacturer's shop window

Omkar Labels has made clothing labels, hang tags and tapes since 1999. Buyers check the website before they pick up the phone.

  • What they make: labels, trims, tags and packaging.
  • Whether to trust it: two factories, in Thane and Narmadapuram, and a four-point quality check on every order.
  • Proof: OEKO-TEX, FSC and Global Recycled Standard certifications.
WhyA broken page or an old listing costs trust with exactly the people about to call.
02Plan

Information architecture

Organize the site around the buyer's questions

A B2B buyer needs to confirm what Omkar makes and whether to trust it, then ask for a price, without calling first.

  • What do you make? Products grouped into 4 families, from the label inside the collar to the box on the shelf.
  • Can I trust you? About Us and Quality one tap away, with the factories and certifications.
  • How do I order? A 4-step path: order intake, design, quality check, delivery.
  • Request a Quote as the one primary action, so the next step is always visible.
03Check · me

Design-to-code handoff

Raise the mismatch before release

When the design file and the browser didn't match, I raised it early and we solved it together before release. When a layout broke in one browser and not another, I traced the cause in Chrome DevTools and fixed the code, not the symptom.

WhyI've been the developer on the other side of a handoff, so my own handoffs now spell out states, spacing and behavior.
04Build

Responsive front-end

From design file to working page

  • Layout: HTML and CSS, with Bootstrap's grid so pages held up on every screen.
  • Behavior: JavaScript and jQuery.
  • Content: I kept the catalog of 50+ products accurate as things changed, on the company's WordPress site.

01
Context

A Manufacturer's Shop Window

Omkar Labels makes labels for other businesses, and its customers include major US retail chains. For a B2B manufacturer the website does a specific job: it is where a buyer checks what the company makes before picking up the phone. A product page that renders wrong, or a listing that is out of date, costs trust with exactly the people the company needs to impress.

02
The Build

From Design File to Working Page

The designer produced the interface; I turned it into pages that worked. That meant building responsive layouts in HTML and CSS, adding behaviour in JavaScript and jQuery, and using Bootstrap's grid so pages held together on every screen size, all inside a WordPress site.

Alongside new pages I maintained the product listing pages across a catalog of more than 50 products, keeping them accurate and consistent as products changed.

03
Working With the Designer

Where Designs Meet Reality

Not everything in a design file survives contact with a browser. I worked directly with the designer on feasibility, raising the places where the file and the build would not match, and resolving them together before release rather than shipping a compromise nobody had agreed to.

When layouts broke in one browser and not another, I traced the cause in Chrome DevTools and fixed it in the code, instead of patching over the symptom.

04
What It Changed

Why a Designer Should Have Built Things

This is the role that shaped how I design now. Having been the developer receiving the file, I know which questions a handoff leaves unanswered and which details get lost on the way to production. It is why my handoffs specify states, spacing and behaviour instead of leaving them to be guessed, and why I can talk through HTML and CSS implementation with front-end developers on their terms.

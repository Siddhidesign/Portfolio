1Step 1 of 6 · Context · Business and user goals

The website is the manufacturer's shop window

Omkar Labels has made clothing labels, hang tags and tapes since 1999. Buyers check the website before they pick up the phone.

  • What they make: labels, trims, tags and packaging.
  • Whether to trust it: two factories, in Thane and Narmadapuram, and a four-point quality check on every order.
  • Proof: OEKO-TEX, FSC and Global Recycled Standard certifications.

Why it matters: a broken page or an old listing costs trust with exactly the people about to call.

3Step 3 of 6 · Build · Design-to-code handoff, responsive design

From design file to working page (2022)

As a part-time front-end developer, I developed the website's pages in code, turning the designer's files into responsive pages on the company's WordPress site.

  • Layout: HTML and CSS, with Bootstrap's grid so pages held up on every screen.
  • Behavior: JavaScript and jQuery.
  • Content: I kept the catalog of 50+ products accurate as things changed.
  • Handoff: when the design file and the browser didn't match, I raised it early and we solved it together before release.
  • Debugging: when a layout broke in one browser and not another, I traced the cause in Chrome DevTools and fixed the code, not the symptom.

Why it matters: I've been the developer on the other side of a handoff, so my own handoffs now spell out states, spacing and behavior.

4Step 4 of 6 · Rebuild · Accessible design, form design, SEO

Six decisions, and the principle behind each

I no longer had the 2022 code, so in 2026 I rebuilt the site from the designer's Figma files in Next.js 16, React 19 and TypeScript. I planned and directed the build, reviewed the code and tested every page. An AI coding assistant wrote much of the code.

  1. Decision 1 · Forms

    Catch mistakes before the quote is sent

    A quote request with a typo is a lost lead. The form checks every field in the browser, then again on the server. An invalid form stops before sending, and focus jumps to the first problem.

    UX principles

    • Error prevention. Problems are caught before the request leaves.
    • Help users recognize and recover from errors. Each message sits under its own field, and editing a field clears its error.
  2. Decision 2 · Accessibility

    Every error is tied to its field

    Each message is linked to its input, so a screen reader reads the error together with the field.

    UX principles

    • Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA). The same feedback for people who can't see the red text.
  3. Decision 3 · Navigation

    A phone menu you can always escape

    On tablets and phones, the menu opens full screen. Esc closes it, the close button gets focus first, and the page behind it can't be reached by mistake.

    UX principles

    • User control and freedom. A clear, keyboard-friendly way out.
    • Consistency and standards. Built on the browser's own dialog, so it behaves the way people expect.
  4. Decision 4 · Content

    An FAQ that shows one answer at a time

    Questions open one at a time, and work even without JavaScript, with the keyboard and screen readers handled by the browser.

    UX principles

    • Progressive disclosure. Buyers see the questions first, and open only the answer they need.
    • Aesthetic and minimalist design. A short page instead of a wall of text.
  5. Decision 5 · Findability

    Pages search engines can read

    B2B buyers often start with a search. Every page has a title, description and canonical link, the home page adds structured business data, and 5 of 6 pages are served as plain HTML.

    UX principles

    • Findability. The site shows up where the buyer's journey starts.

    BuiltIt's a preview, so there's no traffic to report.

  6. Decision 6 · Responsive

    Design tokens from the Figma file

    Colors, type and spacing are variables taken from the designer's file. Spacing steps down at 1100px and 768px, between the 1440px desktop and 390px phone frames.

    UX principles

    • Consistency and standards. One source for every color and space, so pages match the design.
    • Flexibility. The same content works from phone to desktop.
5Step 5 of 6 · Test · Automated accessibility audit, contrast check, code review

Checked, not assumed

Before calling the rebuild done, I checked it at phone and desktop sizes.

  • Accessibility: axe-core against WCAG 2.1 AA, on all 6 pages.
  • Contrast: text over photos, checked against the worst spot behind it.
  • Code quality: TypeScript in strict mode and ESLint.

Limit: automated checks find many accessibility problems, not all. Next would be testing with a screen reader user and real buyers.

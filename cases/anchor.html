An AI budgeting coach people trust

Tapping a card doesn't feel like spending, so small buys and forgotten subscriptions add up. For our master's capstone, our team of 4 designed Money Anchor, a budgeting app with an AI coach that speaks up before you spend and explains why.

1Step 1 of 7 · Problem

Money that doesn't feel real

Cards and digital wallets remove the small pain of paying, so spending feels effortless until the statement arrives. Forgotten subscriptions keep charging. And most budgeting apps only chart the past, which many people avoid opening.

Why: So the aim was to help in the moment, before the money is gone, without judging.

2Step 2 of 7 · Research

24 people, 50+ quotes

We read 40+ papers on spending behavior and benchmarked 10 budgeting apps, including Mint, YNAB, Rocket Money and Copilot. Then we interviewed 8 people aged 20 to 32 and ran 4 focus groups with 16 more, with university ethics (IRB) approval.

  • People don't need more graphs. They need money in context, like "this meal costs 3 hours of work."
  • Forgotten subscriptions were a big, silent leak.
  • People would trust AI only if it explained itself and left the final say to them.

Why: Interviews covered private money habits. Focus groups surfaced the social side, like spending to keep up with friends.

3Step 3 of 7 · Ideas

100 ideas, down to 10

Each of us wrote 25 ideas, 100 in all. We sorted them into 7 themes, cut duplicates down to 64, then voted on each from "Not feasible" to "Must have". The top 10 went into the prototype, like spotting unused subscriptions and AI forecasts for savings goals.

Why: We kept the wild ideas too. A card that locks itself when you seem stressed was out of reach, but it pointed to the real need: a gentle nudge before you buy.

4Step 4 of 7 · Structure

Five sections, like a bank app

We sketched by hand, then wireframed 5 sections with a bottom bar, so each one is a tap away. Every screen was tied to a research finding.

Why: A layout like their bank's matched how people already picture their money, so there was less to learn.

5Step 5 of 7 · Design

An AI that explains, and leaves you in charge

In Figma, we turned the wireframes into high-fidelity screens built from shared components. I designed the AI coach and the budget flows.

Key decisions
  1. Key decision 1

    Explain every suggestion

    The thinking: People wanted the reason and the final say, not orders. So each AI suggestion shows why, and how sure it is, before you apply it or say no.

    TestedIn round two, 75% completely trusted the AI's budget advice.

  2. Key decision 2

    Help before you buy

    The thinking: Spending decisions happen in the moment, not in a monthly report. So you can ask the AI before a purchase, and it compares products, prices and stores within your budget.

    TestedRound one: everyone finished the shopping task, and testers said it would save them time.

  3. Key decision 3

    A nudge, not a lecture

    The thinking: People said apps that scold them make them avoid money altogether. So alerts are gentle, and the AI suggests while you decide.

    Tested88% said an alert would make them rethink or cancel a purchase.

6Step 6 of 7 · Testing

Two rounds, then fix what failed

We ran 2 rounds of moderated tests on Maze and Zoom, 8 people per round. Each person explored the app, acted on AI advice, adjusted a budget and asked the AI for buying advice, thinking out loud while Maze recorded success, time and misclicks.

What testing changed
  1. Changed after testing

    The AI gets its own button

    The thinking: In round one, the AI sat in the bottom menu and people missed it. It became a chat bubble you can open from any screen, and later got a gradient so it stands apart from the menu.

    TestedRound two: 100% finished the AI chat task.

  2. Changed after testing

    Buttons that say what they do

    The thinking: Testers misread the plus and minus icons, and the home screen felt crowded. We removed the quick-action buttons and labeled the main ones Adjust Income and Add Expense.

    TestedRound two: 100% success and 0% misclicks exploring the app.

  3. Still open after testing

    Still hard: changing a budget

    The thinking: In round two, adjusting a budget still caused 54.7% misclicks, because people couldn't find where to start. We added Adjust to a menu on each budget.

    Not yet testedChanged after round two; not tested again.

7Step 7 of 7 · Results

What changed, and what I learned

  • 22% → 100%success exploring the app
  • 0%misclicks on it in round two
  • 75%completely trusted the AI's advice
  • 88%would rethink a purchase after an alert
What I learned

  • Watch, don't just ask

    Testers rated navigation 4.4 out of 5 while still missing buttons. Behavior told the real story.

  • If they can't find it, it can't help

    The AI only worked for people once it was visible from every screen.

  • Trust takes time

    People wanted suggestions, not orders, and checked bigger decisions themselves.

01
Context

A Product Growing Faster Than Its System

FindMe is a consumer product built by a small team moving quickly. Features shipped every sprint, and each new screen was a chance for the interface to drift: a slightly different button, a new spacing value, a pattern solved twice in two different ways.

That is the problem a design system exists to prevent, and it is also the one that is easiest to postpone when the roadmap is full. My job was to keep the system growing at the same pace as the product, so consistency came from the library rather than from someone catching mistakes in review.

02
Design System

Components, Patterns and Standards

I added reusable components, interaction patterns and visual standards to FindMe's design system library in Figma. The aim was practical: when a new feature needed a card, a form or a list, the answer should already exist in the library, with its variants and states defined, instead of being redrawn from scratch.

Keeping files, patterns and naming consistent mattered as much as the components themselves. A library that engineers cannot navigate does not get used, and a component that is not used does not keep anything consistent.

03
Feature Design

From Discovery to Delivered UI

Alongside the system work I designed responsive web and mobile interfaces end to end: discovery and research first, then wireframes, then interactive prototypes, then the final UI. Designing responsive from the first wireframe meant the mobile layout was a decision, not an afterthought squeezed out of the desktop version.

04
Working With Engineering

Inside the Sprint

I worked inside the agile team with engineering and product, joining backlog refinement and sprint planning rather than handing designs over the wall at the end. Being in those conversations meant design questions surfaced while they were still cheap to answer.

Handoff was built to be read by developers directly: components referenced by their library names, states specified, and implementation worked through with the developers building it, so the shipped screens matched the designed ones.

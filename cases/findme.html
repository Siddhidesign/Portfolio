Growing a design system while the product kept shipping

FindMe helps people build a portfolio and share one link. Over a summer, I grew its Figma component library to 25+ components across 2 products, held brand pages to the brand's color, type and icon standards with Marketing, and designed features from research to final UI, inside the sprint with engineering.

1Step 1 of 6 · Problem

A product growing faster than its system

FindMe helps people build a career portfolio and share one link. New screens shipped every sprint, which meant small drift: a slightly different button, a new spacing value, the same pattern solved twice.

Why: Drift slows engineers down and makes a product feel stitched together.

2Step 2 of 6 · Approach

Two tracks at once

Over a summer internship, I worked on two tracks: adding components, patterns and visual standards to the Figma library, and designing features from research to final UI.

  • When a feature needed a card, a form or a list, the answer was already in the library.
  • Every component came with its variants and states, so nothing was left to guess.
  • With Marketing, brand-facing pages kept the same color, type and icon standards, and engagement on them grew 28%.
  • I supported 12+ user interviews and usability tests in Maze, and fed what we learned into the wireframes.
3Step 3 of 6 · System

A library engineers can navigate

A library only helps if people can find things in it and trust what they find.

Key decisions
  1. Key decision 1

    Name components the way engineers look for them

    The thinking: A library engineers can't navigate doesn't get used, so every name follows one pattern: Button / Primary / Medium / Default.

    ShippedEvery handoff used the library names.

  2. Key decision 2

    Define every variant and state

    The thinking: When nothing is left to guess, built screens match designed ones. Every component had its default, hover, focus, disabled and loading states defined.

    ShippedI worked with the developers until the built screens matched.

4Step 4 of 6 · Features

Research to final UI

For each feature, I went from research to wireframes, interactive prototypes and the final UI, all in Figma. Every screen was responsive from the first wireframe.

Why: Starting responsive made the phone layout a decision, not an afterthought.

5Step 5 of 6 · Sprint

Working with engineering

I worked inside the same sprints as engineering and product, not ahead of them.

Key decision
  1. Key decision 3

    Design inside the sprint

    The thinking: I joined backlog refinement and sprint planning, so design questions came up while they were still cheap to answer. Handoffs named components by their library names and spelled out every state.

    ShippedThe library kept 2 products consistent as new screens shipped.

6Step 6 of 6 · Results

In the founder's words

In numbers: a library of 25+ components kept 2 products consistent, brand pages designed with Marketing grew engagement 28%, and 12+ user interviews and usability tests shaped the wireframes.

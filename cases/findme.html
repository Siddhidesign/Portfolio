01Input

The problem

A product growing faster than its system

New screens shipped every sprint, which meant small drift: a slightly different button, a new spacing value, the same pattern solved twice.

WhyDrift slows engineers down and makes a product feel stitched together.
02Data in

Research

Research fed the wireframes

I supported 12+ user interviews and usability tests, tagged the findings in Dovetail, and fed what we learned into the wireframes.

User interviewsUsability tests in MazeDovetail tagging
03Design

System

A library engineers can navigate

I worked on two tracks at once: adding components, patterns and visual standards to the Figma library, and designing features from research to final UI. Every screen was responsive from the first wireframe.

decision 1shipped

Name components the way engineers look for them

A library engineers can't navigate doesn't get used, so every name follows one pattern: Button / Primary / Medium / Default.

Every handoff used the library names.

decision 2shipped

Define every variant and state

When nothing is left to guess, built screens match designed ones. Every component had its default, hover, focus, disabled and loading states defined.

I worked with developers until the built screens matched.

04Verify · me

Working with engineering

Design inside the sprint, not ahead of it

I joined backlog refinement and sprint planning, so design questions came up while they were still cheap to answer. Handoffs named components by their library names and spelled out every state.

With Marketing, brand-facing pages kept the same color, type and icon standards.

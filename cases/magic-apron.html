#HD-2026run 07 of 07conceptAI assistant · retail concept

Ask the right question, finish the task

I watched a first-time DIYer ask The Home Depot's AI assistant, Magic Apron, how to hang a photo frame. In Store Mode it showed only hanging kits, so they had to ask again. When the item wasn't on the shelf, reporting it ended with "Thanks for letting us know." My redesign asks the 2 questions that matter as taps, and turns an empty shelf into a way to finish the task.

01Data in

Research

Same question, same person, two answers

I interviewed a first-time DIYer while they used the app, and recorded the screen. They asked the same question on the Home tab and in Store Mode: "I have a 24×28 photo frame and need to hang it on a wall. What products should I buy?"

  • Home tab: a hanging kit, or adhesive strips for no holes.
  • Store Mode: hanging kits only. Strips appeared only after they typed "Adhesive."
  • Magic Apron did ask about weight and wall type, but at the end of a long paragraph, so they read past it.

Then the map showed the strips at Aisle 15, Bay 10. They weren't there. Reporting it showed "Thanks for letting us know!" and nothing else: no similar item, no other store, no delivery.

02Analyze

Define

Two problems, two questions to answer

Problem 1

First-time DIYers have to ask again, because the questions are buried in text and Store Mode narrows results without asking.

How might we

let the AI ask the 1 or 2 questions that matter, as quick taps, before it picks products?

Problem 2

Shoppers can report an empty shelf, but the app ends at "Thanks for letting us know."

Why it mattersThe store gets a useful signal, but the shopper still has no product.
03Verify · me

My check

Check each idea against the real screens

I wireframed the whole journey in grayscale first. It changed too much of the app, so I kept the ideas and moved them onto the real screens, checking each change against a usability principle.

04Design

Before and after

Six small changes on the real screens

change 1

Ask the most important question first

Before showing any product, Magic Apron asks how you want to hang it. In my interview, this one question would have caught "adhesive."

Error prevention
change 2

Questions as taps

Questions move out of the paragraph into their own card, 1 of 2 then 2 of 2, with a quiet "Skip questions" at the bottom.

Recognition over recall
change 3

The right items, with the aisle

Results repeat the shopper's own answers, no holes and 10 to 20 lb, with the aisle and bay.

Match the real world
change 4

Low stock? Offer pickup today

When fewer than 5 are left, "Buy for pickup" opens a simple sheet with the photo, price and pickup place, and a "Not now" button.

User control and freedom
change 5

A visible "Can't find it?" button

A labeled button on the product card replaces the small three-dot menu.

Fitts's law
change 6

Report, then finish the task

After the thank-you, "Still need it today?" offers a similar item, a nearby store or delivery, with Undo.

Help users recover from errors
05Result

How I'd measure it

How I'd measure it

These are hypotheses, not results. I'd watch:

  • How often people ask again in the same chat
  • How often people answer the questions or skip them
  • Pickup orders on low-stock items
  • "Can't find it" reports that still end in a purchase
LimitsOne interview plus my own walkthroughs is a signal, not proof. Next, I'd test with 5 first-time DIYers, at home and in the store.

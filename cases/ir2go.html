Making the IRS app easy to use

01Input

The problem

A government app people gave up on

People open IRS2Go to check a refund, pay what they owe, or find free tax help. All three were hard: forms rejected correct details, payments bounced out to the website, and help didn't cover refunds or payments.

2.8 ★App Store rating
45%quit before finishing a task
0original screens met WCAG 2.1 AA
WhyWhen a tax app confuses people, they start to doubt the agency behind it.
02Data in

Research

Reviews, Quora, interviews and a survey

I mined 150+ App Store reviews and 200+ Quora posts, interviewed about 10 taxpayers and ran a survey. We watched people try each task in the old app, checked it against 10 usability rules, and summarized related research papers with AI.

150+App Store reviews
200+Quora posts
~10interviews, plus a survey
03Analyze · AI

Synthesis

Write out every step, find where it breaks

We wrote out every step of each task to see exactly where people got stuck.

  • Refunds: Tax Year looked like a text box but was a list, the SSN was split across 3 boxes, and the refund amount had to match to the cent.
  • Payments: the app sent people to the IRS website and through several more pages, and some still couldn't pay.
  • Tax help: it explained ways to file, but nothing about refunds or payments.
WhyWriting out every step showed the cause of each failure, so we fixed causes instead of symptoms.
04Design

Decisions log

Every task finishes inside the app

Clear labels, one main action per screen, big tap targets, and 3 clear choices on Home instead of 7 equal menu items: Refunds, Payments and Tax Help.

decision 1 · error preventiontested

Help before the error

The old form explained its rules only after people got them wrong. Now every field has a label and an example, and a sample form shows where each answer is on your return.

Part of the redesign that scored 78.25.

decision 2 · system statustested

The answer as a timeline

"Return Received" gave no date and no next step, and 68% of people left the old flow without a clear answer. Now the status reads Received, Approved, Sent.

Part of the redesign that scored 78.25.

decision 3tested

Pay without leaving the app

The old app sent people out to the website in the middle of paying. Now each payment option sits in the app, with its fees side by side.

All three tasks finish inside the app.

05Verify · me

Usability testing

Old app against new, then fix

People did the same tasks on the old app and the redesign while thinking out loud. Then they rated each version with the System Usability Scale (SUS), a standard 10-question survey scored out of 100.

old app31.3
redesign78.25
common average68
changed after testingtested

Guests first

Many people quit at the login screen; they wouldn't log in just to check a refund. So "Explore without signing in" became the main button.

Nobody dropped off at login after the change.

changed after testingtested

A summary before paying

3 of 6 testers hesitated to pay because they couldn't see a summary first. We added a confirmation screen with the total before the pay button.

After the change, 0 of 6 were worried about paying.
